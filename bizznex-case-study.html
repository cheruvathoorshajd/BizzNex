02

§ The problemWhy redraw it

Why the default looks AI-generated

The original BizzNex frontend was assembled from stock component libraries (Fluent UI, MUI) with drop-in cards, gradients and rounded boxes. It worked — and looked like every other tool built the same way. The root cause is distributional convergence: the industry defaults (Tailwind / shadcn) share one habit — containerization. Every element becomes a bordered, shadowed, rounded card, so screens collapse into a grid of near-identical boxes.

The brief was therefore not “pick nicer colours” but change the grouping mechanism. The thesis that carried every later decision:

Group with space, type and hairlines — never boxes. Delete every border and the structure should still read.

03

§ InputsResearch

Research & north stars

Three research passes

  • Why AI UIs look generic — the containerization habit; the fix is editorial grouping.
  • Container-less, editorial layout — named-line grids, whitespace (Gestalt proximity), hairline rules, asymmetric splits, bleed and overlap; data in borderless tables with tabular numerals.
  • Systems audit — how restrained products carry density without boxes.

North stars

  • Mercury, Linear, Attio, Stripe, Anthropic — data-dense but human.
  • AREA 17, RoAndCo — editorial studios, for type and grid discipline.
  • Colophon credit, verbatim: “Distilled from Mercury, Linear, Stripe & Anthropic.”

One radius. Only marks (dots, avatars) round. Flat by default; one soft shadow for overlays, windows, the tour card and the toast.

Motion

  • Spatial: things travel to and from where they belong. Dialogs grow out of the button you pressed, windows come from the sidebar and go into the dock, and dark mode spreads from its switch. Switching pages and the big moments use transition screens (§ 08).
  • Three curves: --ease cubic-bezier(.22, 1, .36, 1) for states, --ease-sp cubic-bezier(.2, .8, .2, 1) for travel, --spring for the toast.
  • One speed dial: every duration is multiplied by --m: 1.5 (MOTION_SCALE in ui/motion.ts), so the whole product calms down or speeds up together.
  • Static blocks never animate; prefers-reduced-motion turns all of it into instant changes.

Signature details

  • Folio numbers in faint serif open every section (01–13 here).
  • § mono spec-labels in accent-keyed uppercase — the editorial fingerprint.
  • 5% film grain — a fixed SVG fractal-noise overlay removes the flat “screenshot” feel.
  • Accent-tinted thin scrollbars; ::selection uses the accent.

§ Interaction modelWindowing

“Use every feature at once” — Studio

A parallel exploration asked how a business owner could run many tools at once. Four prototypes shared the Aurora palette; only the interaction layer differed.

ShellSidebar + main66px rail + canvasSidebar + mainSidebar + main
Open triggerL-click inline widget / R-click windowClick = windowClick = toggle panelL-click full page / R-click window

VerdictRight idea, cramped inlineToo much — loses readable pagesToo littleBuilt
  • Left-click a tool → its full-page version opens inline, like Dashboard or Deals, with an “Open in window ↗” button in the top bar.
  • Right-click a tool → a floating window with the tool’s compact widget, not the full page — a focused work surface vs a glanceable utility.
  • Windows open at a uniform 280 × 380, drag by the title bar, resize, minimise and close; focus raises them. On phones they go full-width.
  • The dock appears with the first window and disappears with the last. A sidebar dot marks tools with an open window.
  • Workspace and CRM pages stay inline-only — data views need the full page.

Why this split

  • Skyline is the brand beat — the mark draws itself and its one window lights: the business opening its doors. Saved for the four ways in, so it never becomes wallpaper.
  • Aurora carries the rest — the same mesh that sits behind the landing page and the theme switch, blooming quietly for goodbyes, endings and page changes.
  • Fade through paper — words leave, then the glow, then the page appears. The calmest exit: it never competes with the content arriving, reads the same in both themes, and costs nothing to render.
  • Switching features replaced the old sideways page slide, so moving around the product and the big moments share one language.

Explored and set aside

  • Ledger — an editorial sentence over the app’s outline. The best fit for Aurora’s rules, but it gave the ways in no sense of arrival.
  • Aurora’s first exit — the app opening out of a circle. Replaced: too theatrical for something seen many times a day.
  • Settle, Lift, Gather — the glow sinking, rising like a veil, or condensing into a point where you land. Gather was the most “spatial”; paper won on calm.

Rules for every screen

  • No flash — a wait under 300 ms shows nothing; once shown, a screen holds long enough not to blink.
  • Say what is happening — every screen names the action; the status line is aria-live.
  • Reduced motion — a short cross-fade with the same words; switching features becomes instant.
  • One at a time — a request made during a screen just applies its change underneath.
  • One scale — every duration is multiplied by the ×1.5 motion scale.

Implementation: apps/web/src/ui/transitions.tsx (playTransition covers the screen, makes the change underneath, holds, then exits) and styles/transitions.scss. Timings at ×1.5: Skyline 1.2 s in · 0.72 s out; Aurora 0.72 s in · 0.93 s out; switching features 0.3 s in · 0.5 s hold · 0.57 s out. The full study, with every option, is design-style-guides/bizznex-transitions.html.

§ Implementationapps/web · apps/api

How it ships

The design is not a mockup — it is the running app. Here is what implements each decision.

Stack

  • Web — React 18, TypeScript, Vite; Redux Toolkit and RTK Query; react-intl; one hand-written aurora.scss; three.js for the dashboard globe.
  • API — NestJS 11, Prisma, PostgreSQL, JWT auth with revocable sessions; shared Zod 4 contracts in @bizznex/shared.
  • Dropped Fluent UI, MUI, framer-motion and styled-components — they impose the card idiom the brief rejects.
  • No outside AI service. Brenda answers from the user’s own records with no API key and no network calls.

Data model

  • Workspace — Project, Task, Note.
  • CRM — Company, Contact, Deal (Lead · Qualified · Proposal · Won · Lost), Activity (Call · Email · Meeting · Note · Task).
  • Tools — Post, Campaign, Event, Notification. Only the server writes notifications: a deal won, a campaign sent, a post published. Scheduled campaigns go out when they're next read, so no job runner is needed.
  • Onboarding — one JSON field on the user, merged by PATCH /onboarding; sample data via POST / DELETE /onboarding/sample.

App shell

  • 248px sticky sidebar with three groups — Workspace · CRM · Tools — and a user footer. It folds to a 64px icon rail (the toggle by the logo, or [); while folded, hovering or tabbing into the rail pops the full sidebar out over the page without moving it. The choice is kept per browser.
  • Top bar: breadcrumb, search on Projects, Tasks and every CRM page, “Open in window ↗”, EN / ES / FR, theme, and the ? Help menu.
  • Window state in layout/useWindows.ts; drag geometry stays inside each window to avoid re-renders.
  • Each tool has a full page (registry.tsx) and a compact widget (widgets.tsx).

Cross-cutting

  • Theming — data-theme on <html> flips the token set, set before first paint; Light, Dark or Auto (follows the OS), saved per browser.
  • i18n — navigation, the dashboard, projects and tasks in English, Spanish and French; the CRM, tools, settings, landing, sign-in and onboarding are English for now.
  • Responsive — breakpoints at 900 / 860 / 640 / 480px; the sidebar becomes a drawer, windows go full-width.
  • Accessibility — visible focus rings, labelled icon buttons, keyboard-driven tour (← → Esc), dialogs and tool windows that take and return focus, muted text at WCAG AA contrast, and error boundaries around every page and window.

Delivery

  • Images — multi-stage Docker builds on Node 22: the API runs as a non-root user and applies migrations on start; the web app is static files.
  • CI — every push runs the typecheck, lint, unit and end-to-end tests against PostgreSQL, then builds both images and boots the API in production mode.
  • Hosting — free and always live: Neon Postgres, the API on Render kept awake by a ping on /health/live, the web app on Render’s CDN (render.yaml).
  • Security — the Content-Security-Policy ships inside the page with its script hash computed at build; rate-limited sign-in; sessions revocable everywhere.

The logo: Skyline

The name stays BizzNex. The logo is Skyline: three towers standing on one baseline with the name, and one lit window, the light that says the business is open.

The signature lockup, with the horizon arc and the tagline. In the product the logo appears without the arc: in the sidebar, on sign-in, in the setup wizard, on the landing page and as the browser-tab icon.

The decision

QuestionDecisionWhat else was considered
NameKeep BizzNex. Its “Nex” already carries the Latin nexus, a binding together: the whole business connected.Dozens of Latin, Hebrew and “Nex”-family names, each checked against existing companies. Finalists included Statio, Oriens, Limen, Diurna, Innexa and AuxiNex.
LogoSkyline, developed from the founder’s own reference drawing.Arch and Rise (from the same reference); Lift, Ledger and Bloom (an earlier proposal); and an eight-mark picker.
ColourBizzNex colours: ink on warm paper, with the window in Aurora violet, so the logo matches the app.The reference’s cream on black with an orange window, kept as an alternative.
WordmarkOutfit Bold, tracked −2%, with the “zz” tightened to −3.5%.Fraunces serif, Plex Sans capitals, Plex Mono lowercase.

From the reference

  • Kept: buildings for business, one baseline under the mark and the name, the horizon arc, a heavy geometric name, a spaced-capital tagline.
  • Fixed: five or six line weights became one; perspective that blurred below 64px became flat fronts on a grid; the crowded “zz” was re-tracked; the tagline became BizzNex’s own.

Construction

  • A 48-unit grid with one stroke of 2.6 units (5.4%) everywhere.
  • Tower heights 17 · 24 · 31: even steps of 7 units.
  • One lit window in the tallest tower, the only colour in the mark.
  • The baseline runs one unit past the towers and continues under the name at the same weight.
  • The 16px favicon drops the window stripes and thickens the stroke; the lit window stays.
