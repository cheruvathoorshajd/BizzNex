Your skyline, drawn properly

The reference has the right instincts: architecture for business, a single line drawing, one baseline under everything, a horizon arc, a strong geometric name. Below is what I kept, what I fixed, and three directions that turn it into a logo that works from a 16px browser tab to a shop sign.

✓ Kept from the reference

  • Buildings for business. A clear, friendly metaphor anyone gets in a second.
  • One baseline under mark and name. It ties the two into a single unit.
  • The horizon arc, now with a job: it is the sun of the next day, and it sets the clear space.
  • A heavy geometric name, a spaced-capital tagline, and one accent colour. The reference used orange on black; BizzNex sets it in its own colours, with the lit window in Aurora violet.

→ Fixed

  • Line weights. The drawing mixes five or six; every direction here uses one stroke on a strict grid.
  • Small sizes. The perspective buildings blur below 64px; each direction has a symbol that holds at 16px.
  • Spacing. The “zz” crowds; the name is re-tracked and the baseline aligned to the drawing.
  • Words. “Content creation, simplified.” describes another product, and “Microservices” is engineering talk; the tagline is BizzNex’s own, Business, run beautifully.

Recommendation

Recommendation: Skyline. It stays closest to the reference you liked, fixes everything that would break in real use, and adds one detail that gives it a story: a single lit window in the tallest tower, the light that says the business is open.
